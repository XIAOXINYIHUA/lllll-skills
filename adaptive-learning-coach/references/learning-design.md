# Learning Design Reference

Load this reference when building assessments, interpreting evidence, or replanning a course.

## Capability Model

Define capabilities as observable actions rather than content labels.

Weak: “understand joins”  
Strong: “choose and write the correct join for a two-table analysis, explain unmatched rows, and debug duplicate amplification.”

Use four states:

| State | Evidence |
|---|---|
| new | No valid evidence yet |
| fragile | Correct only with cues, or inconsistent |
| working | Independent success in a familiar context |
| mastered | Independent success plus transfer or delayed retrieval |

A numeric mastery estimate supports prioritization but does not replace the evidence label.

## Diagnostic Design

Use a small assessment with increasing depth:

1. **Recall** — name, recognize, or define.
2. **Explanation** — explain a relationship or predict an outcome.
3. **Application** — solve a familiar task.
4. **Transfer** — solve a changed task or choose among methods.
5. **Correction** — find and fix a plausible error.

Stop early when the user's level is clear enough to place the starting milestone. Diagnostics are for placement, not exhaustive grading.

## Session Sizing

For a 30-minute session, use approximately:

- 5 minutes retrieval;
- 7 minutes micro-lesson;
- 8 minutes guided practice;
- 8 minutes independent transfer;
- 2 minutes reflection and scheduling.

For longer sessions, add another practice cycle or project work. Do not proportionally lengthen explanation.

## Score Interpretation

Suggested rubric for a task scored from 0 to 100:

- correctness: 40;
- method, reasoning, or process quality: 20;
- independence: 20;
- transfer to the specific context: 15;
- self-check and correction: 5.

Adjust weights when the goal requires speed, fluency, style, or safety.

## Review Scheduling

The state script uses a conservative SM-2-inspired schedule:

- score below 60: reset repetitions; review tomorrow;
- score 60–79: short interval, usually 2 days;
- score 80–89: grow through roughly 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days;
- score 90 or above: grow faster only when independent transfer was observed.

Override automatic dates when a deadline is closer, a prerequisite blocks progress, or the learner requests an intensive review.

## Adaptation Patterns

### Correct but slow

Keep the concept active. Add fluency practice, chunking, and time-boxed retrieval; do not reteach the whole concept.

### Wrong with high confidence

Ask for the rule or mental model that produced the answer. Contrast it with a minimal counterexample, then request a corrected prediction.

### Correct with low confidence

Ask for a brief explanation and schedule another retrieval soon. Avoid unnecessary reteaching.

### Repeated failure

Change representation, reduce complexity, test prerequisites, and provide a worked example followed by a faded example. If two approaches fail, split the capability.

### Rapid success

Use a harder transfer task, compress later repetition, and move saved time to projects or weak topics.

## Plan Quality Checklist

A strong plan has:

- a concrete outcome and deadline;
- explicit assumptions;
- capability-level mastery criteria;
- authentic milestone deliverables;
- diagnostic and final assessments;
- retrieval distributed across sessions;
- at least 20% buffer;
- a policy for falling behind;
- a definition of done.
